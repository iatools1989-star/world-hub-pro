## Roteiro completo (inglês, ~9 min)

### [0:00] Hook

"Tell me about yourself."

`[VISUAL: a pergunta aparece em texto gigante, com som de notificação]`

Four words that open almost every interview — and the moment where most candidates lose the job in the first two minutes. They recite their resume. They talk about their dog. They say "well, what do you want to know?" Today I'm giving you the exact formula hiring managers want, plus three word-for-word scripts you can adapt before your next interview. Bookmark this one, because you'll come back to it.

### [0:52] A fórmula

First, understand why they ask. They don't want your life story. They're testing three things: can you communicate clearly, is your background relevant to this role, and do they actually want to work with you for eight hours a day.

`[VISUAL: três ícones — communication, relevance, likability]`

Your answer needs to run 60 to 90 seconds — any longer and you're monologuing — and it follows a structure I call Present, Past, Future.

`[VISUAL: três blocos na ordem PRESENT → PAST → FUTURE]`

Present: who you are right now and what you do. Past: the experience that got you here, with one concrete result. Future: why this specific job is your next step. That last part is the secret — most people never connect their story to the company, and that's what makes a hiring manager lean forward.

### [2:10] Script 1 — Entry level

Here's the first full script, for an entry-level candidate with no full-time experience. Imagine you're applying for a junior marketing coordinator role.

`[VISUAL: o texto do script rola na tela em blocos, destacando Present/Past/Future]`

"I'm a recent communications graduate focused on digital marketing and content creation. During college I ran the Instagram account for our student marketing club, where I grew followers from 200 to over 900 in one semester by posting three times a week and testing different content formats. I also completed a certification in Google Analytics and used it to analyze traffic for a class project, where my team presented a growth plan to a local nonprofit. Now I'm looking for an entry-level marketing role where I can apply those content and analytics skills, and this coordinator position caught my attention because your team just launched that new social campaign — I'd love to help build on that kind of work."

Notice what that does: present identity, a past story with numbers, and a future tied to something real about their company. No hobbies, no birthplace, no rambling.

### [4:05] Script 2 — Career changer

Script two, for career changers — and this is harder, because you have to explain the pivot without sounding lost.

`[VISUAL: duas setas de duas áreas diferentes se encontrando no nome da empresa]`

"I'm currently an elementary school teacher making a transition into customer success. Teaching trained me to read an audience, handle difficult conversations with empathy, and explain complex things simply — skills I saw matter most when I spoke with two customer success managers about their roles. Last year I volunteered to manage communications for our school's parent association, which meant coordinating 40-plus families, resolving conflicts, and tracking every request in a spreadsheet — essentially managing a client pipeline. What excites me about this role at your company is that your product serves educators, so I bring firsthand knowledge of the customer I'd be supporting. I'm looking for a team where communication skills are the core of the job, and that's exactly what customer success is."

The pattern: transferable skill first, proof second, bridge to the company third. Never apologize for changing fields — reframe it as an advantage.

### [5:55] Script 3 — Phone screen

Script three is the 30-second version, because phone screen interviews move fast and recruiters talk to ten candidates a day.

`[VISUAL: timer de 30 segundos na tela]`

"I'm a junior marketing coordinator with about a year of hands-on experience in social media and content analytics. Before that I studied communications and ran marketing for my university club, growing their audience fourfold. I'm job searching now because I want a role with more analytics responsibility, and this position's focus on data-driven content is exactly the direction I want to grow."

Same structure, half the length. If they want more detail, they'll ask — and now you're in a conversation, not a monologue.

### [7:00] Cinco erros fatais

Now the five answers that quietly kill your chances.

`[VISUAL: lista numerada com X vermelho em cada item]`

One: reciting your resume word for word — they can read. Two: leading with personal life — "I'm a mom of two who loves hiking" belongs nowhere near the first 60 seconds. Three: saying you have no experience, even in an entry-level interview — you have projects, volunteering, and coursework, and script one just showed you how to use them. Four: speaking for more than two minutes straight. And five: making it all about what you want — "I'm hoping to gain experience" — instead of what you deliver. Flip it: here's what I can do for you.

### [8:15] Tailor em 5 minutos

Here's how to tailor this to any job in five minutes. Open the job description, find the two responsibilities listed first, and make sure your past section proves you've done something related — even at a smaller scale. Then find one specific fact about the company: a recent launch, a value on their careers page, a product update. That one fact goes into your future section. Specificity is the difference between a memorized answer and a convincing one.

### [9:00] Método de prática + CTA

Finally, practice with the three-times method. First, write your answer out. Second, read it out loud three times until it sounds like speech, not a script. Third, record yourself on your phone and watch it once — you'll instantly spot the rambling and the filler words. Do that tonight, and this question goes from scary to the easiest part of the interview.
